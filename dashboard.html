        Metric Glossary

        Each entry in the dataset is scored across 21 attributes. Below is what each metric represents and how to interpret it.

        Metric Type Description
        Role TitleTextThe specific job title or career path being evaluated.
        Country PathCategoryThe immigration and employment pathway. "High-Risk: USA" reflects strong earning potential but difficult visa/PR processes. "Safe: AUS/NZ" and "Safe: CAN" offer easier permanent residency but typically lower compensation.
        Masters DegreeTextThe recommended or required graduate degree to enter this role competitively. Indicates the academic investment needed.
        Masters Cost (USD)DollarEstimated total tuition cost for the required masters program in that country. Includes tuition only, not living expenses.
        Admission Competitiveness1 – 10How difficult it is to gain admission to the required masters program. 10 = extremely selective (top-5 programs, sub-5% acceptance rates). 1 = widely accessible.
        Direct Hire Likelihood1 – 10Probability of receiving a full-time offer upon completing the degree. 10 = near-guaranteed placement through structured pipelines. Lower scores indicate a more competitive or fragmented job market post-graduation.
        Global Employers CountIntegerApproximate number of companies globally that actively hire for this role. A proxy for market breadth and job liquidity. Higher counts mean more opportunities and less concentration risk.
        Interview Competitiveness1 – 10Difficulty of the interview process once you land one. 10 = multi-round technical gauntlets typical of quant/FAANG. 1 = straightforward behavioral interviews.
        Fresher Salary (USD)DollarExpected total compensation in year one (base + bonus + equity where applicable). Represents the median for candidates with a fresh masters degree.
        5-Year Salary (USD)DollarExpected total compensation after 5 years of experience, assuming normal career progression.
        10-Year Salary (USD)DollarExpected total compensation at the 10-year mark. Typically reflects senior individual contributor or early management level.
        20-Year Salary (USD)DollarExpected compensation at 20 years. Represents staff/principal-level IC roles or director-level management.
        Max Salary (USD)DollarThe realistic salary ceiling for top performers at the peak of this career track. Not an average; represents the upper bound (e.g., P90-P99 outcomes).
        Fresher Volatility1 – 10How unstable the job market is for entry-level candidates. 10 = highly cyclical, layoffs common for juniors, hiring freezes frequent. 1 = steady demand regardless of market conditions.
        Senior Stability1 – 10Job security and career durability at the senior level (10+ years). 10 = near-impossible to displace, deep institutional knowledge required. 1 = even seniors face significant displacement risk.
        PR Difficulty1 – 10How difficult it is to obtain permanent residency in that country through this career. 10 = extremely difficult (e.g., USA H-1B lottery + multi-year green card backlog). 1 = straightforward pathway.
        PR Timeline (Years)FloatEstimated time from starting work to receiving permanent residency. Factors in visa processing, employer sponsorship timelines, and country-specific backlogs.
        AI Displacement (20yr %)PercentProjected percentage of this role's current responsibilities that could be automated by AI within 20 years. Higher values indicate greater long-term disruption risk. Does not mean job elimination, but significant scope reduction.
        Remote Work1 – 10Feasibility of performing this role fully remote. 10 = entirely digital, asynchronous-friendly. 1 = requires physical presence (labs, hardware, on-site operations).
        Undergrad Fit1 – 10How well a typical undergraduate computer science or engineering curriculum prepares you for this role. 10 = direct pipeline from undergrad coursework. Lower scores indicate the role requires significant domain-specific knowledge not covered in standard programs.
        Cognitive Alignment1 – 10How well this role matches a systems-thinking, problem-solving cognitive profile. 10 = the role fundamentally rewards structured analytical thinking, debugging complex systems, and architectural reasoning. Lower scores indicate roles that lean more on soft skills, relationship management, or domain memorization.

        Role Directory

        All 45 evaluated roles, grouped by domain. Each description covers what the role does, where it sits in industry, and what makes it distinct.

        Simulation & Real-Time 3D

        Synthetic Data Env Artist
        MS CompSci (Graphics)
        Builds photorealistic 3D environments used to generate synthetic training data for machine learning models. Works at the intersection of computer graphics and AI, creating scenes that teach autonomous systems to perceive the real world without real-world data collection. Critical for self-driving, robotics, and computer vision pipelines.
        Sim2Real Tech Director
        MS Robotics Eng
        Leads the technical strategy for transferring robot behaviors trained in simulation to physical hardware. Oversees the simulation fidelity, physics modeling, and domain randomization techniques that bridge the gap between virtual and real-world performance. A senior leadership role in robotics R&D organizations.
        Digital Twin Architect
        MS Systems Eng
        Designs and maintains real-time digital replicas of physical systems (factories, cities, aircraft, power grids). These twins ingest live sensor data and enable predictive maintenance, scenario planning, and optimization without disrupting actual operations. Spans manufacturing, energy, defense, and smart infrastructure.
        Virtual Prod Tech Artist
        MS Tech Art (VFX)
        Bridges the gap between creative direction and real-time rendering technology in film and broadcast production. Builds LED volume workflows, real-time compositing pipelines, and Unreal Engine-based virtual sets. The technical backbone behind productions that shoot actors in front of massive LED screens displaying photorealistic CG environments.

        Spatial & Interactive Systems

        Spatial Computing Strategist
        MS HCI / Spatial Tech
        Defines product and platform strategy for augmented, virtual, and mixed reality experiences. Works on how spatial interfaces will replace or augment traditional screens. Evaluates hardware capabilities, interaction paradigms, and market readiness for spatial computing products at companies like Apple, Meta, and enterprise AR firms.
        Interactive Systems Designer
        MS Interactive Media
        Architects complex interactive experiences that go beyond traditional UI. Designs systems for real-time collaboration tools, interactive data visualization platforms, immersive installations, and next-generation human-computer interfaces. Combines design thinking with systems engineering.

        Core Systems & Rendering

        Rendering Pipeline Engineer
        MS CompSci (Systems)
        Builds and optimizes the low-level graphics rendering stack: shaders, rasterization, ray tracing, GPU compute pipelines. Works on game engines (Unreal, Unity), film renderers, or GPU driver teams. One of the most technically demanding graphics roles, requiring deep knowledge of GPU architecture and real-time performance constraints.
        Systems & Low-Level C++ Eng
        MS CompSci (Systems)
        Works on operating systems, compilers, databases, game engines, or other performance-critical infrastructure written in C/C++/Rust. Deals with memory management, concurrency, cache optimization, and hardware-software interfaces. The foundation layer that everything else runs on.
        Embedded Systems & Firmware
        MS Electrical & Comp Eng
        Programs the software that runs directly on hardware: microcontrollers, sensors, actuators, and custom silicon. Works in automotive (ECUs), medical devices, consumer electronics, aerospace, and IoT. Requires understanding of electrical engineering, real-time operating systems, and hardware constraints that pure software engineers rarely encounter.

        Software Engineering

        Backend Software Engineer
        MS Computer Science
        Builds server-side application logic, APIs, databases, and distributed systems. The workhorse role of the tech industry with the largest employer base. Works on everything from authentication services to payment processing to content delivery at scale.
        Frontend / Web UI Engineer
        MS Computer Science / HCI
        Develops the user-facing layer of web applications: component systems, state management, rendering performance, accessibility, and cross-browser compatibility. High employer count but faces the most significant AI displacement risk in the dataset due to the structured, pattern-heavy nature of UI code.
        Full-Stack Software Engineer
        MS Computer Science
        Operates across the entire application stack, from database schemas to API design to frontend rendering. Valued for breadth and the ability to own features end-to-end. The most common engineering title at startups and mid-size companies.
        Mobile Applications Engineer
        MS Computer Science
        Builds native or cross-platform applications for iOS and Android. Deals with platform-specific APIs, performance optimization on constrained devices, offline-first architectures, and app store distribution. Faces meaningful AI displacement as mobile UI generation tools mature.
        Machine Learning / Applied AI
        MS Artif Intelligence / CS
        Deploys machine learning models into production systems. Works on training pipelines, feature engineering, model serving, A/B testing, and monitoring. Distinct from ML research; focuses on making models work reliably at scale in real products. High demand but rapidly evolving tooling landscape.

        Infrastructure & Cloud

        DevOps & SRE Engineer
        MS CompSci / Cloud Comp
        Manages the reliability, deployment, and operational health of production systems. Builds CI/CD pipelines, container orchestration, monitoring stacks, and incident response tooling. Site Reliability Engineering (SRE) focuses on uptime guarantees and error budgets. One of the most remote-friendly and in-demand infrastructure roles.
        Data Platform / ETL Engineer
        MS Data Science / CompSci
        Designs and maintains the data infrastructure that powers analytics and ML: data lakes, warehouses, streaming pipelines, and ETL/ELT workflows. Works with tools like Spark, Kafka, Airflow, and dbt. The plumbing that makes data-driven decision-making possible at scale.
        Cloud Infrastructure Strategist
        MS Distributed Systems
        Architects multi-cloud and hybrid cloud strategies for organizations. Evaluates and implements cloud-native services, cost optimization, security postures, and migration paths. Works at cloud providers (AWS, GCP, Azure) or as a principal-level architect at enterprises undergoing cloud transformation.
        Data Center Topology Architect
        MS CompSci / Network Eng
        Designs the physical and logical layout of data center networks: server rack placement, cooling topology, network fabric, and interconnects. Works at hyperscalers (Google, Meta, Microsoft) or colocation providers. Combines networking expertise with physical infrastructure planning.
        Network Fault Tolerance Eng
        MS Network / Reliability Eng
        Designs networks that survive failures gracefully: redundant routing, automatic failover, partition tolerance, and chaos engineering for network infrastructure. Works at ISPs, CDNs, cloud providers, and large enterprises where downtime costs millions per minute.
        Edge Computing Strategist
        MS IoT / Distributed Computing
        Architects systems that push computation from centralized clouds to the network edge: CDN nodes, 5G base stations, IoT gateways, and on-device inference. Critical for latency-sensitive applications like autonomous vehicles, industrial automation, and real-time gaming.

        Industrial Operations & Logistics

        Industrial Sim Architect
        MS Industrial Eng / Robotics
        Builds simulation models of ports, factories, and supply chain facilities. Uses discrete-event simulation and agent-based modeling to optimize throughput, identify bottlenecks, and test layout changes before physical implementation. Works with manufacturing giants, logistics companies, and consulting firms.
        Logistics & Ops Researcher
        MS Operations Research
        Applies mathematical optimization (linear programming, combinatorial optimization, stochastic modeling) to supply chain problems: inventory placement, demand forecasting, route optimization, and warehouse layout. Works at Amazon, FedEx, consulting firms, or in-house supply chain teams.
        Transport Network Planner
        MS Transportation Systems
        Designs and optimizes transportation networks for airlines, public transit systems, freight rail, and urban mobility. Models passenger flow, schedules, capacity, and infrastructure investments. Works with transit agencies, airlines, departments of transportation, and urban planning consultancies.
        Fleet Robotics Traffic Controller
        MS Robotics / Automation
        Orchestrates the movement of fleets of autonomous robots in warehouses, factories, or outdoor environments. Solves multi-agent path planning, collision avoidance, task allocation, and traffic flow optimization. Works at Amazon Robotics, warehouse automation companies, and autonomous delivery startups.
        Warehouse Automation Logic Designer
        MS Systems Eng / Robotics
        Designs the control logic and workflows for automated warehouse systems: conveyor routing, robotic pick-and-place sequencing, sorting algorithms, and packing optimization. Bridges mechanical systems with software intelligence. Critical role as e-commerce drives warehouse automation investment.
        Last-Mile Logistics Routing Eng
        MS Operations Research / CompSci
        Optimizes the final delivery leg from distribution center to customer doorstep. Solves vehicle routing problems (VRP) with time windows, capacity constraints, and dynamic re-routing. Works at delivery companies, food delivery platforms, and e-commerce logistics teams. Combines OR algorithms with real-time systems.
        Automation Integration Specialist
        MS Mechatronics / Automation
        Connects disparate industrial automation systems: PLCs, SCADA, robotic arms, vision systems, and enterprise software. The "glue" role that makes factory automation actually work end-to-end. Strong demand due to the fragmented nature of industrial automation vendors and protocols.

        Infrastructure & Systems Planning

        Infrastructure Capacity & Grid Modeler
        MS Energy Systems / Data Sci
        Models power grid capacity, load forecasting, and infrastructure expansion planning. Works with utility companies, grid operators, and renewable energy developers to ensure supply meets demand. The highest senior stability score in the dataset (10/10) reflects the essential, non-automatable nature of grid planning.
        Healthcare Flow & Systems Architect
        MS Health Informatics
        Designs patient flow systems, hospital capacity models, and healthcare operations optimization. Works on scheduling algorithms, resource allocation, and clinical workflow automation. Employed by hospital systems, health IT companies, and government health agencies.
        Smart City Infrastructure Planner
        MS Urban Tech / Systems
        Plans the technology infrastructure for smart city initiatives: IoT sensor networks, traffic management systems, utility monitoring, and citizen services platforms. Works with municipal governments, consulting firms, and urban tech companies. Combines urban planning knowledge with systems engineering.

        Quantitative Finance

        Systematic Quantitative Researcher
        MS Financial Eng (MFE) / Math
        Develops algorithmic trading strategies using statistical models, machine learning, and alternative data. Works at hedge funds (Citadel, Two Sigma, DE Shaw) and prop trading firms. The highest salary ceiling in the dataset ($3M) but also the most selective admission and interview process. Extremely competitive, with a narrow employer base.
        High-Frequency Execution Architect
        MS Comp Eng / Low-Level Sys
        Builds ultra-low-latency trading infrastructure: FPGA-based order execution, kernel-bypass networking, custom network protocols, and co-located server optimization. Measures performance in nanoseconds. Works at HFT firms (Jump Trading, Virtu, Tower Research) where microsecond advantages translate to millions in profit.
        Derivatives & Commodities Quant Trader
        MS Applied Math / MFE
        Prices and trades complex financial derivatives: options, futures, swaps, and structured products across commodities, rates, and equity markets. Combines deep mathematical modeling (stochastic calculus, PDE methods) with market intuition. The second-highest salary ceiling ($2.5M) but also the highest AI displacement risk (60%) and lowest cognitive alignment score.
        Market Microstructure Analyst
        MS Fin Eng / CompSci
        Studies how orders interact in financial markets: order book dynamics, price formation, market impact modeling, and execution optimization. Works at exchanges, market makers, and institutional trading desks to understand and exploit the mechanics of how markets actually work at the tick level.
        Quant Risk & Portfolio Analyst
        MS Fin Math / Data Sci
        Builds risk models for investment portfolios: Value-at-Risk, stress testing, factor modeling, and regulatory capital calculations. Works at banks, asset managers, and insurance companies. More structured and regulated than pure trading roles, with correspondingly higher AI displacement risk as risk models become increasingly automated.
        Quantitative Developer (Infrastructure)
        MS CompSci (Systems)
        Builds the technology stack that quant researchers and traders depend on: backtesting frameworks, market data systems, execution management platforms, and research infrastructure. The software engineering counterpart to quant research, with more job security and lower compensation variance.
        Cryptoeconomic Mechanism Designer
        MS CompSci / Game Theory
        Designs the economic incentive structures for blockchain protocols and decentralized systems: tokenomics, governance mechanisms, auction designs, and staking models. Applies game theory and mechanism design to create systems where self-interested participants produce collectively beneficial outcomes. High volatility given crypto market cycles.

        Business, Strategy & Management

        Business Operations (BizOps) Manager
        MBA / MS Management Tech
        Sits at the intersection of data analysis, strategy, and execution. Identifies operational inefficiencies, builds dashboards and models, and drives cross-functional initiatives. Common at high-growth tech companies as a hybrid role between product management, finance, and strategy. The MBA degree reflects the business orientation of the role.
        Corporate Strategy & M&A Analyst
        MBA (Finance/Strategy)
        Evaluates acquisition targets, conducts due diligence, builds financial models, and advises on corporate strategy. Works at investment banks, private equity firms, or in-house corporate development teams. The most expensive masters path in the dataset ($110K) reflecting top MBA program costs. High ceiling but significant AI displacement risk for analytical work.
        Technology Strategy Consultant
        MBA / MS Tech Management
        Advises enterprises on technology adoption, digital transformation, and IT strategy. Works at consulting firms (McKinsey, BCG, Deloitte) or as an in-house strategist. Combines business acumen with technical literacy to translate technology capabilities into business outcomes. Large employer base (25,000) reflects the consulting industry's scale.
        Pricing & Revenue Strategist
        MS Econ / Applied Analytics
        Designs pricing models, revenue optimization algorithms, and monetization strategies. Applies econometrics and A/B testing to maximize revenue per user across subscription, marketplace, and advertising business models. Works at SaaS companies, e-commerce platforms, and airlines. High remote work viability.
        Technical Program Manager (TPM)
        MS Eng Management / CompSci
        Drives complex technical programs across multiple engineering teams. Manages dependencies, timelines, risk mitigation, and stakeholder communication for large-scale platform or infrastructure initiatives. A deeply valued role at FAANG companies where the complexity of shipping at scale demands dedicated program orchestration.
        Developer Relations (DevRel) Eng
        MS CompSci / HCI
        Advocates for a platform or API by creating documentation, sample code, tutorials, and conference talks. Bridges the gap between the product engineering team and external developers who build on the platform. Combines technical depth with communication skills. Highly remote-friendly (9/10).
        Frontier Tech VC Analyst
        MBA / MS Innovation Tech
        Evaluates early-stage technology companies for venture capital investment. Conducts technical due diligence, market sizing, and competitive analysis. Requires the ability to assess both the business viability and technical feasibility of emerging technologies. Very small employer base (1,200) but the second-highest salary ceiling ($2M) at the partner level.
        E-Sports Analytics Director
        MS Data Science / Sports Mgt
        Applies data analytics to competitive gaming: player performance modeling, team composition optimization, audience engagement metrics, and tournament economics. A niche role with the smallest employer base in the dataset (400 globally) but growing as e-sports matures into a structured industry with franchise leagues and broadcast deals.
