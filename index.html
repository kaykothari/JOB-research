        Metric Glossary

        Each career path in the database is evaluated across 25 key attributes. Here is a clear, plain-English explanation of what every metric measures and how to interpret the scores.

        Metric Type Plain-English Meaning & Interpretation
        Role Title (Entry / General)TextThe base job title you enter at the start of this career track.
        Mid-Career RoleTextThe title you typically hold around year 5–10 (e.g. Staff Engineer, Engineering Manager, Group PM, or BizOps Director).
        Endgame RoleTextThe peak executive destination at the top of the ladder (e.g. CTO, CEO, CPO, CIO, or VP).
        Primary SkillBadgeThe main daily technical or strategic capability you need to excel (e.g. Coding, Strategy, Machine Learning, Optimization).
        Secondary SkillBadgeThe critical supporting capability that accelerates promotions into leadership (e.g. Systems Architecture, Financial Modeling, Cross-Functional Leadership).
        Country PathCategoryWhere you work and immigrate. "High-Risk: USA" offers highest compensation but tough H-1B/green card rules. "Safe: AUS/NZ" and "Safe: CAN" offer reliable permanent residency pathways.
        Masters DegreeTextThe graduate degree that gives you the highest placement probability and best compensation leverage.
        Masters Cost (USD)DollarEstimated tuition for the master's program in that country (tuition only, excluding living costs).
        Admission Competitiveness1 – 10How hard it is to get into top master's programs (10 = elite Ivy League/top-5 with sub-5% acceptance; 1 = accessible).
        Direct Hire Likelihood1 – 10Odds of receiving full-time job offers immediately upon graduation (10 = on-campus recruiting pipelines; 1 = difficult job hunt).
        Global Employers CountIntegerApproximate number of companies globally hiring for this role. Higher numbers mean more job mobility and safety against company layoffs.
        Interview Competitiveness1 – 10Difficulty of landing the job once invited to interview (10 = multi-round LeetCode hard and math tests; 1 = conversational behavioral rounds).
        Fresher Salary (USD)DollarExpected total compensation in year 1 after completing your master's degree (base pay + bonuses).
        5-Year Salary (USD)DollarExpected total compensation after 5 years of solid performance and promotions.
        10-Year Salary (USD)DollarExpected total compensation at the 10-year mark (senior IC, engineering manager, or director level).
        20-Year Salary (USD)DollarExpected total compensation around 20 years in (Director, VP, or Principal level).
        Max Salary (USD)DollarRealistic salary ceiling for top performers at the peak of the field (top 5-10% outcomes).
        Fresher Volatility1 – 10How risky the job market is for juniors (10 = vulnerable to layoffs and hiring freezes during recessions; 1 = steady hiring year-round).
        Senior Stability1 – 10Career durability once you have 10+ years of experience (10 = nearly impossible to displace due to deep domain expertise; 1 = high replacement risk).
        PR Difficulty1 – 10Difficulty of obtaining permanent residency / green card in that country through this job (10 = high lottery backlogs like US H-1B; 1 = smooth point system).
        PR Timeline (Years)FloatAverage estimated years from your first work day until permanent resident status is granted.
        AI Displacement (20yr %)PercentProjected percentage of routine day-to-day responsibilities that AI could automate over the next 20 years.
        Remote Work1 – 10Feasibility of doing this job fully remote (10 = completely asynchronous & digital; 1 = physical presence in labs, trading desks, or factories mandatory).
        Undergrad Fit1 – 10How well a standard undergraduate computer science or engineering degree prepares you without needing drastic pivots.
        Cognitive Alignment1 – 10How much the job rewards logical systems thinking and technical problem solving vs. social networking or memorization.

        MS Computer Science
        Primary: Coding Secondary: Systems Architecture

You start out writing code, advance into leading engineering teams or designing large systems, and can end up as Chief Technology Officer (CTO) running all company technology.
